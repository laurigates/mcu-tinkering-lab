# RCWL-1601 Ultrasonic Rangefinder (EC Buying listing, 3–5 V)

The ultrasonic rangefinder on robocar-unified, `[parts.ranger_front]` and
`[parts.ranger_rear]` in its `hardware.toml`. The robot carries two, front
(fixed) and rear, both run in I2C mode (see [Interface mode](#interface-mode)). One 4-pin
header on the bottom edge, between the transmitter and receiver. For the module's electrical and timing facts see
[`sensor--rcwl-1601.md`](../datasheets/sensor--rcwl-1601.md).

**The fitted module:** sold as "RCWL-1601 Ultrasonic Sensor 3-5V for Arduino",
AliExpress item [1005001622236950](https://www.aliexpress.com/item/1005001622236950.html)
(listing photos watermarked "EC Buying"). Transducer face: `RCWL-1601`, the RCWL
logo, `T` under the left transducer and `R` under the right. Back face: chip
`U2` (16 pins), a three-lead part `U1`, header `J1`, and the mode pads `R5`/`UART`
and `R4`/`IIC`. The chip is marked `RCWL-9600` in listing photo 5, though the
listing advertises CS100A compatibility — see the datasheet page.

## Physical layout

Viewed from the transducer face, `RCWL-1601` at the top: the transmitter (`T`)
on the left, the receiver (`R`) on the right, the header on the bottom edge.
The SMD parts and the header's plastic body are on the other face, and the
pins are a **right-angle** header running parallel to the board out past the
bottom edge (listing photos 3 and 4).

`Side`/`Pos` follow the convention in
[`tools/hardware/layout.py`](../../../tools/hardware/layout.py): `L`/`R`/`T`/`B`
edge, `Pos 1` at the left of a top or bottom edge. Pin names are the I2C-mode
functions the robot uses; the silkscreen prints the GPIO-mode names in mixed
case alongside the alternate-mode names, given in `Silkscreen`. Both
rangefinder parts in `hardware.toml` name this page, and their `SCL`/`SDA` join
against it through `[[bus_nets]]` on mux channels 3 and 4. `Header` is `J1` as
printed on the back face; no pin-1 mark is visible in the photos, so no pad
number is claimed.

| Pin | Side | Pos | Header | Silkscreen | Notes |
|-----|------|-----|--------|------------|-------|
| VCC | B | 1 | J1 | `Vcc` | 3.0–5.5 V; robocar-unified feeds 3V3 |
| SCL | B | 2 | J1 | `Trig` / `SCL` / `Rx` | I2C clock; TRIG in GPIO mode |
| SDA | B | 3 | J1 | `Echo` / `Tx` / `SDA` | I2C data; ECHO out in GPIO mode |
| GND | B | 4 | J1 | `Gnd` | |

Outline 40 × 18 mm (dimension arrows in listing photos 1 and 6). Four corner
mounting holes. Adafruit's dimension drawing for its own RCWL-1601 (#4007)
gives 37 mm × 15 mm between hole centres, a 1.6 mm dimension at a corner hole
(apparently its diameter), and 14.69 mm from the left-hand holes to the first
header pad; that drawing is for Adafruit's board, not this one. Header pitch
scales to ≈2.6 mm in listing photo 6 and ≈2.6 mm in Adafruit's drawing, both
consistent with 2.54 mm; neither source states it.

## Interface mode

**robocar-unified runs both rangefinders in I2C mode: fit 10 kΩ on `R4`
(`IIC`) and leave `R5` (`UART`) empty.** The module then answers at 7-bit
address **0x57**, which cannot be changed. The read protocol is on the
datasheet page.

`R5` and `R4` are both unpopulated in the listing photos, so the module ships
in trigger/echo (GPIO) mode and must be modified. Two sources support the
I2C fit:

- **Adafruit #4742**, Adafruit's I2C-mode RCWL-1601, is the same PCB. Its
  back-face product photo (`4742-02.jpg`) shows the same `RCWL-9600` /
  `20200402` chip as `U2`, `R5` empty, and a chip resistor on `R4` marked `103`
  (10 kΩ), printed rotated.
- **Olimex SNS-RCWL1604**, a sibling board with mode pads `M1`/`M2`: both open
  is GPIO, 10 kΩ on `M1` alone is I2C at 0x57, 10 kΩ on `M2` alone is UART,
  both is 1-Wire. Different labels, so it corroborates the value rather than
  naming this board's pads.

Two warnings from the Adafruit #4742 product page, verbatim:

- "there's no built-in pullup resistors". The carrier must pull SCL and SDA
  up on each rangefinder's bus.
- "it doesn't like to share the I2C bus, we think it gets confused by other
  commands and can lock up the bus". For that reason each rangefinder sits
  alone on its own TCA9548A channel: channel 3 and channel 4. Two units at the
  same fixed address could not share a bus in any case.

With both rangefinders on I2C, XIAO D2 and D3 no longer carry TRIG and ECHO;
D2 drives the TCA9548A `RST` instead.

## Source

Read from the AliExpress listing's silkscreen photos, **not** from a vendor
board file, and **not bench-verified with a continuity meter**. No board file
is published for this module. `.claude/rules/board-layout-from-vendor-files.md`
names a product photo as an unreliable source: the labels are legible, but a
mirrored read looks right too. Here the header labels are printed on the
transducer face only, beside the pads, and the back-face photo shows `J1` on
the same (bottom) edge, which is consistent with the order above. The listing
is for a product line, not for the unit in the robot.

## Check on the physical board before a carrier is ordered

- [ ] The pin order: with the transducer face up and `RCWL-1601` at the top,
      confirm with a continuity meter that the rightmost pad (`Gnd`) is the
      ground pour and the leftmost (`Vcc`) is not, and trace `Trig` and `Echo`
      to `U2`.
- [ ] Pitch (2.54 mm assumed) and the first pad's distance from the left edge.
- [ ] Hole spacing and diameter against Adafruit's 37 × 15 mm drawing.
- [ ] Header style: right-angle as in the listing, or straight. A right-angle
      header points the pins away from the board's bottom edge, which decides
      whether the carrier takes a socket or a cable.
- [ ] `R4` and `R5` are both empty as delivered; after fitting 10 kΩ on `R4`
      only, `R5` is still empty.
- [ ] The chip marking on `U2` (`RCWL-9600`, `CS100A` or other). The I2C fit
      relies on it matching Adafruit #4742's `RCWL-9600`.
- [ ] On 3V3 with pull-ups on SCL/SDA, an I2C scan finds the module at 0x57
      and nothing else.
- [ ] A reading against a known distance, and the shortest wait between the
      `0x01` trigger and the read that still returns a fresh value (see the
      datasheet page; the sources range from "a few milliseconds" to 120 ms).
